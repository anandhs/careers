## What this job is

Chemical engineers design processes for producing, transforming, and transporting chemicals, materials, and energy. They apply principles of chemistry, physics, mathematics, and engineering to solve problems related to the production or use of chemicals.

## Day in the life

A typical day for a chemical engineer may involve working in a lab to conduct experiments, collaborating with a team on design projects, and analyzing data to improve production processes. They often spend time reviewing project plans and reports, meeting with clients, and ensuring compliance with safety regulations.

## Earning potential

The earning potential for chemical engineers varies widely based on experience, education, and industry, but in the US, salaries typically range from $85,000 to $130,000 with the potential for growth as they gain experience.

## How to get there

To become a chemical engineer, you generally need a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering or a related field. Internships during college can also provide valuable experience and connections in the industry.

## A course plan through high school and college

### Grades 9-10
Focus on foundational courses in chemistry, physics, and mathematics. Consider participating in science fairs and math clubs to develop critical thinking skills.

### Grades 11-12
Take advanced courses in chemistry, calculus, and physics. Participate in relevant extracurricular activities like engineering clubs and summer science programs.

### College years
Major in chemical engineering. Courses should include thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, reactor design, and process control. Internships or co-op positions provide hands-on experience in real-world engineering practices.

## Skills that help

Key skills for chemical engineers include problem-solving abilities, strong analytical skills, proficiency in mathematics and science, and effective communication skills. Additionally, familiarity with computer software used in engineering simulations is advantageous.

## Leading companies

Leading companies in the chemical engineering field include Dow Chemical, DuPont, and Chevron Phillips Chemical. These firms provide opportunities for career growth and innovation in various sectors.

## Risks and tradeoffs

Chemical engineers frequently face risks related to safety in laboratories and industrial environments. They must adhere to strict health and safety codes and may work in high-pressure situations. Additionally, the work can be demanding and occasionally requires long hours.

## Next 10 year outlook

The outlook for chemical engineers is positive, with a projected growth rate of about 4% in the next decade. Demand for chemical engineers will be driven by the need for energy-efficient processes and sustainable practices.

## What to know before choosing it

Pursuing a career in chemical engineering requires strong dedication to both mathematics and science. It's essential to consider your interests in technology and environmental issues, as these factors will play a prominent role in your work.
