## What this job is

Chemical engineers design and develop processes for converting raw materials into valuable products. They apply principles of chemistry, physics, and engineering to solve problems related to the production or use of chemicals.

## Day in the life

A typical day for a chemical engineer may include conducting experiments in a laboratory, analyzing data using software tools, collaborating with other engineers or scientists, and troubleshooting processes in manufacturing facilities. They may also need to prepare reports and presentations to communicate their findings.

## Earning potential

Chemical engineers in the US can expect to earn between $85,000 and $120,000 annually, depending on their experience and the industry they work in. In Europe, salaries may vary widely, with some roles offering competitive packages in countries like Germany and the Netherlands.

## How to get there

To become a chemical engineer, you typically need a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering or a related field. Gaining internship experience during college can be invaluable. Some positions may prefer or require a master's degree for advanced roles or specialization.

## A course plan through high school and college

### Grades 9-10  
Focus on foundational science and math courses such as Chemistry, Physics, and Advanced Mathematics. Participate in science fairs or engineering clubs to gain practical experience.  

### Grades 11-12  
Take advanced placement (AP) courses in Chemistry, Calculus, and Physics. Consider enrolling in dual-enrollment courses at local colleges for engineering or science credits.  

### College years  
Pursue a Bachelor's degree in Chemical Engineering. Key courses include Thermodynamics, Fluid Mechanics, Reaction Engineering, and Process Design. Participate in internships or co-op programs to gain real-world experience.

## Skills that help

Key skills for chemical engineers include problem-solving, analytical thinking, teamwork, and communication. Proficiency in software tools like MATLAB or Aspen Plus can also be advantageous.

## Leading companies

Notable companies hiring chemical engineers include BASF, Dow Chemical, and DuPont, among others, which offer a range of opportunities in industries from pharmaceuticals to renewable energy.

## Risks and tradeoffs

Chemical engineering can involve working with hazardous materials and may require safety precautions. Additionally, job availability can be influenced by economic conditions, and engineers may need to continuously update their skills due to technological advancements.

## Next 10 year outlook

The demand for chemical engineers is expected to grow in the next decade, particularly in fields like biotechnology, materials science, and environmental engineering, driven by emerging technologies and a shift towards sustainable practices.

## What to know before choosing it

Prospective chemical engineers should be prepared for a rigorous educational background and a continuous learning mindset. It's crucial to enjoy problem-solving and working with complex processes and systems.
