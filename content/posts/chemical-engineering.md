## What this job is

Chemical engineers apply principles of chemistry, biology, physics, and mathematics to solve problems involving the production or use of chemicals. They develop processes for large-scale manufacturing, plan and test production methods, and oversee the production of a variety of materials and chemicals.

## Day in the life

A chemical engineer typically begins their day reviewing project timelines and objectives. They might conduct experiments, analyze data, and collaborate with team members on process improvements. Meetings with project managers and presentations of findings to stakeholders are common, along with potential site visits to manufacturing plants.

## Earning potential

According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the median salary for chemical engineers in the U.S. is approximately $116,000 per year. Salaries can range significantly based on experience, location, and specific industry.

## How to get there

To become a chemical engineer, you typically need a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering or a related field. Internships during college can provide valuable experience. Some engineers also pursue graduate studies to advance their knowledge and career prospects.

## A course plan through high school and college

Grades 9-10: Focus on foundational courses in math (Algebra, Geometry) and science (Chemistry, Biology). Participate in STEM clubs or science fairs.

Grades 11-12: Take advanced math courses (Pre-Calculus, Calculus) and AP Chemistry if available. Seek out internships or summer programs related to engineering or science.

College years: Enroll in a Bachelor’s program in Chemical Engineering. Key courses include Thermodynamics, Fluid Mechanics, Reaction Engineering, and Process Design. Consider involvement in engineering organizations and expos to network.

## Skills that help

Key skills for chemical engineers include strong analytical abilities, problem-solving skills, proficiency in mathematics, and strong communication skills. Familiarity with engineering software and programming languages can also be beneficial.

## Leading companies

Some of the leading companies in the chemical engineering industry include Dow Chemical, BASF, ExxonMobil, and DuPont, which offer various opportunities for recent graduates.

## Risks and tradeoffs

The field of chemical engineering can involve working with hazardous materials and requires strict adherence to safety protocols. Job prospects can also be affected by economic downturns, and working hours may be long on project deadlines.

## Next 10 year outlook

The demand for chemical engineers is projected to grow 4% over the next decade, driven by the need for new products and processes. Emerging fields such as renewable energy and biotechnology may offer new career opportunities.

## What to know before choosing it

Before pursuing a career in chemical engineering, consider your interest in math and science, as these are crucial to the profession. Be prepared for rigorous coursework and a commitment to safety and ethical standards in practice.
