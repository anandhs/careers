## What this job is

A plumber installs, repairs, and maintains piping systems in residential, commercial, and industrial settings. This includes water supply lines, drainage systems, and appliances such as sinks and toilets.

## Day in the life

Plumbers typically start their day at a designated job site, assessing the plumbing needs for that particular project. They go through blueprints, gather needed materials, and sometimes meet with clients to discuss issues. Throughout the day, they might be repairing leaks, installing new fixtures, or troubleshooting drainage problems. Their work can be physically demanding, requiring them to crawl under homes or navigate tight spaces.

## Earning potential

In the U.S., plumbers can earn between $45,000 and $85,000 annually, depending on expertise, location, and type of employment (self-employed vs. employed by a company). Experience and specialization in areas such as gas plumbing can increase earning potential.

## How to get there

To become a plumber, one typically starts with a high school diploma or GED. Many then pursue a plumbing apprenticeship that combines hands-on training with classroom education. Certifications may also be required depending on the state. It’s advisable to check local requirements before starting a plumbing career.

## A course plan through high school and college

### Grades 9-10
- Focus on foundational subjects: Math, Physics, and General Science.
- Consider joining a vocational program if available.  

### Grades 11-12
- Take courses like Advanced Math and Technical Drawing.
- Participate in after-school trade clubs or projects that involve plumbing or construction.
- Seek summer internships or job shadow opportunities with local plumbing companies.  

### College years
- Enroll in a community college or trade school plumbing program, if available.
- Complete courses in plumbing systems, building codes, and safety regulations.  
- Begin an apprenticeship through a union or company to gain practical experience.

## Skills that help

Key skills for plumbers include problem-solving, attention to detail, physical stamina, and manual dexterity. Good communication skills are also essential for interacting with clients and collaborating with other contractors.

## Leading companies

Some of the leading companies in plumbing include Roto-Rooter, ARS Rescue Rooter, and Baker's Plumbing, which provide a range of plumbing services and often hire apprentices and newly licensed plumbers.

## Risks and tradeoffs

Plumbing can be physically demanding and sometimes dangerous due to exposure to hazardous materials and the need to work in confined spaces. Additionally, the job may require being on-call for emergency repairs, which can lead to irregular hours.

## Next 10 year outlook

The job outlook for plumbers is positive, with ongoing demand expected due to population growth, aging infrastructure, and the need for energy-efficient plumbing systems. Job growth is projected to be around 4% from 2024 to 2034, which is roughly average across all occupations.

## What to know before choosing it

Plumbing can be a rewarding career, offering stability and the potential for self-employment. However, it requires hard work, dedication, and the ability to deal with challenging environments.
